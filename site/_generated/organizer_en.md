One window sold the tickets for every drink. With the tickets in hand, people stood in line again to exchange them. The ticket line, the cocktail line and the two beer lines ran from the bar toward the stage, and the cocktail and beer lines reached the lighting and drone booth in the middle of the floor. At 15:30 buying was relatively quick, and between 18:00 and 20:00 the exchange line took more than 40 minutes; we saw that. We did not measure the longest wait. We rebuilt that night in a computer simulation, and what follows are estimates. The night is reproduced only with 2 to 5 people serving beer. Between 18:00 and 20:00 the simulation estimates about 52 people in the ticket line (between 21 and 121) and 83 in the exchange lines (between 50 and 180). After 20:00 we stopped trying to buy. For 22:00 the simulation estimates 96 minutes in the exchange line (between 56 and 155).

## Serving faster

In 2026 every beer was opened and poured into a cup, and a large beer took two. We tried other ways of serving on the same 64 simulated nights, with 2,000 tickets. Nobody measured how much time each one saves, so the seconds in the table are our assumptions.

With closed cans and faster payment the recommended crew is 8 stations at the bar and 6 sellers on the floor, about 51 fewer working hours per night than serving in cups, and it depends less on sellers: 6 in place of 12.

Handing over closed cans depends on the venue's rules and on security policy about cans on the floor. Draft beer needs cold kegs, gas and space at the bar, and the venue may not be equipped for it; ask before deciding. Timing a few sales at the next edition would show how much each option really saves.

## How this was done

A computer model moves 2,000 attendees through the hall from 13:00 to 03:00 on the official running order. Each person arrives, watches their bands, gets thirsty, picks a line and gives up after waiting too long. What nobody measured (how much people drink, how long serving takes) was tried in 8,960 simulated nights, and the ones that reproduce what we saw in 2026 were kept: a relatively quick purchase at 15:30, more than 40 minutes in the exchange line between 18:00 and 20:00 (and no more than 70, the limit we assumed), long lines at both the ticket window and the exchange, and a lot of people giving up. Each change was evaluated on 48 of those nights. The details are in the technical pages.

One window sold the tickets for every drink. With the tickets in hand, people stood in line again to exchange them. The ticket line, the cocktail line and the two beer lines ran from the bar toward the stage, and the cocktail and beer lines reached the booth of the lighting and drone engineers in the middle of the floor. At 15:30 buying was relatively quick, and between 18:00 and 20:00 the exchange line took more than 40 minutes; we saw that. We did not measure the longest wait. We rebuilt that night in a computer simulation, and what follows are estimates. The simulation reproduces the night with 2 to 4 people serving beer. Between 18:00 and 20:00 the simulation estimates about 52 people in the ticket line (between 20 and 115) and 86 in the exchange lines (between 50 and 179). After 20:00 we stopped trying to buy. For 22:00 the simulation estimates 93 minutes in the exchange line (between 55 and 151).

With closed cans and faster payment the recommended crew is 8 stations at the bar and 6 sellers on the floor, about 48 fewer working hours per night than serving in cups, and it depends less on sellers: 6 in place of 15. If cans save 8 seconds per beer, the recommended crew is 6 stations at the bar and 12 sellers on the floor (186 working hours); if they save 20, it is 6 stations at the bar and 9 sellers on the floor (160 working hours).

Handing over closed cans depends on the venue's rules and on security policy about cans on the floor. Draft beer needs cold kegs, gas and space at the bar, and the venue may not be equipped for it; ask before deciding. Timing a few sales at the next edition would show how much each option really saves.

## How this was done

A computer model moves 2,000 attendees through the hall from 13:00 to 03:00 on the official running order. Each person arrives, watches their bands, gets thirsty, picks a line and gives up after waiting too long. What nobody measured (how much people drink, how long serving takes) was tried in 8,960 simulated nights, and the ones that reproduce what we saw in 2026 were kept: a relatively quick purchase at 15:30, more than 40 minutes in the exchange line between 18:00 and 20:00 (and no more than 70, the limit we assumed), long lines at both the ticket window and the exchange, and a lot of people giving up. Each change was evaluated on 64 of those nights. The ranges on this page cover 9 of 10 simulated nights. The details are in the technical pages. Results of 24 November 2026.
